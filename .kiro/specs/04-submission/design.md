# Design: 04 Submission and pitch

## 1. Judging evidence map (2025 criteria; update on 13 Nov)
| Criterion | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Impact | Whole-Earth search surfaces unstudied candidates for Artemis-era south-polar regions and Mars sites; open, reproducible, free to run; Sarawak education angle |
| Creativity | Per-dimension "best match" cards; target-to-Earth dual view; novelty index; divergence panel as a first-class output |
| Validity | Physical harmonisation (matched baselines, overpass-matched thermal, pooled scaling); rediscovery recall vs random with bootstrap CIs; stability under weight uncertainty; coverage always shown |
| Relevance | Directly answers every challenge objective (see `product.md` table): new analogues, what is and is not analogous, multiple dimensions, Earth's limits |
| Presentation | Live static app, 30-second story, clean visuals, honest numbers |

## 2. Demo storyboard (≈ 30 s)
1. (0–5 s) "Analogue sites are a handful of famous places. We searched all of Earth." Global heat map appears.
2. (5–12 s) Click an Artemis south-polar landmark → five cards animate in.
3. (12–20 s) Open the best *novel* card → scorecard radar + divergence panel ("Gravity, vacuum, radiation: not replicable; thermal gap 240 K").
4. (20–26 s) Switch lens to Cold regions & volatiles → map and cards change.
5. (26–30 s) 3D globes with arcs (or 2D if not built) + closing line: "Every land tile scored, validated against known sites, honest about the gaps."
Numbers on screen must be real pipeline outputs.

## 3. Slide outline (≤ 7)
1. Problem and challenge objective · 2. Approach (fingerprint → score → cards) · 3. Data and harmonisation · 4. Validation (recall@k chart vs random) · 5. Discoveries (2–3 novel candidates with caveats) · 6. Limitations and divergence · 7. Impact, Sarawak, next steps.
